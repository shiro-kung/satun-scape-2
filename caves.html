Into the limestone

Caves

11 boat- and trail-accessible cave systems carved through Satun's karst hills, across several districts.

10 spots listed Karst cave systems

Caves

Phu Pha Phet Cave

Manang By road, then a short walk to the entrance

Best Time to Visit: Year-round

What to Know:

  • One of the largest caves in Thailand, featuring stalactites and stalagmites.
  • Proper footwear and a guide are recommended.

Tham Le Stegodon

Thung Wa By road, then kayak through the cave tunnel

Best Time to Visit: November to April

What to Know:

  • Thailand's longest water cave, rich in fossils.
  • A guided boat tour is required to explore the cave.

Tham Chet Kot (Seven Bends Cave)

Manang By road, then boat or kayak inside the cave

Best Time to Visit: Year-round

What to Know:

  • A winding cave with stunning rock formations.
  • Requires basic fitness for exploration.

Urai Thong Cave

La-ngu By road, walkway inside the cave

Best Time to Visit: November to April

What to Know:

  • Part of the Satun UNESCO Global Geopark, in Ban Urai, Kamphaeng Subdistrict, La-ngu District—fossils of ancient marine life and land animals have been found here.
  • A roughly 90-metre limestone hill with three connected chambers and a walkway built for visitors.
  • Local legend ties the name (from Malay for "gold") to a sunken treasure ship said to have once anchored nearby, when the area was still sea.

Tham Chorakhe (Crocodile Cave)

Mueang Satun Longtail boat or kayak from Ao Pante, Ko Tarutao

Best Time to Visit: November to April

What to Know:

  • A mangrove-canal cave on Ko Tarutao island, reached by longtail boat or kayak from the park headquarters at Ao Pante—despite the name, there are no crocodiles left today.
  • The boat ride winds through mangrove forest before a boardwalk leads to the cave entrance; bring a torch, as the interior is dark.
  • Usually combined with a Tarutao day trip or overnight stay, since it's only reachable by boat.

Tham Rakang Thong Park

Manang By road; some caves require a local guide

Location: 3WV9+V35, Palm Phatthana, Manang District, Satun 91130, Thailand

Details:

  • A forest park with a natural cave amid lush surroundings and large trees, popular for a quiet nature walk.
  • Google rating: 3.8 stars.

Talu Cave

La-ngu By road; some caves require a local guide

Location: WRW9+C67, Kamphaeng, La-ngu District, Satun 91110, Thailand (road entrance)

Details:

  • A natural cave reached via a dirt road through rubber plantations, with a small forest and mushroom-covered interior shown by a local guide.
  • Google rating: 5.0 stars.

Tham Wang Khram (ถ้ำวังคราม)

La-ngu By road; some caves require a local guide

Location: Unnamed Road WRQ8+G4P, Tambon Khao Khao, Amphoe La-ngu, Chang Wat Satun 91110, Thailand

Details:

  • A large, dark cave in the Satun Geopark known for unusually beautiful stalactite and stalagmite formations.
  • Google rating: 4.5 stars.

Tham Phaya Bangsa (Phaya Bang Sa Cave)

Khuan Don By road; some caves require a local guide

Location: Q2GR+2QW, Tambon Khuan Don, Amphoe Khuan Don, Chang Wat Satun 91140, Thailand

Details:

  • A roughly 200-metre cave reached via an unmarked dirt path, with tall ceilings and mostly flat ground.
  • Google rating: 4.3 stars.

Half Heart Cave (Tham Lod Phop Rak)

La-ngu By road; some caves require a local guide

Location: Near Ko Khao Yai / Khao Yai checkpoint, La-ngu District, Satun, Thailand

Details:

  • A flooded limestone sea cave reachable by boat and kayak, notable for a natural stone bridge/arch that forms a heart shape when viewed at low water, located within the Satun UNESCO Global Geopark area near Tarutao/Ko Khao Yai.
  • Google rating: 4.8 stars.
