Emergency & safety

Real, verified numbers for Satun and Thailand-wide emergencies — bookmark this page or take a screenshot before you head out to the islands, where signal can be weak.

In any life-threatening emergency in Thailand, the single most useful number for a foreign visitor is the Tourist Police at 1155 — English-speaking, available 24 hours, and able to relay your situation to local police, ambulance, or fire services on your behalf.

Call now

All emergency calls in Thailand are free, even with no credit or SIM.

1155 Tourist Police English-speaking, 24/7 — your first call for almost anything 1669 Ambulance National medical emergency dispatch 191 Police General police emergency line (Thai-language) 199 Fire Fire department 1193 Highway Police Road accidents and breakdowns 1784 Disaster Prevention DDPM — floods, storms, tsunami coordination

Tsunami safety — Andaman coast

Satun is one of six Andaman coast provinces covered by Thailand's tsunami warning network (a network of coastal warning towers). You don't need to wait for an official siren if you notice the natural warning signs yourself:

  • A strong or unusually long earthquake
  • The sea suddenly and noticeably pulling back from the shore

If you notice either sign — move immediately to at least 15 metres above sea level or at least 3 km inland. Don't wait for confirmation. If you're already on a boat at sea when a tsunami is announced, head to deep water rather than back to shore.

Lost passport

Report to the Tourist Police (1155) first for a police report, then contact your embassy — most foreign embassies are based in Bangkok, not Satun.

Water & island safety

Most of Satun's best spots involve a boat ride.

  • Wear a life jacket on every boat trip, even short transfers — operators are required to provide them, ask if you don't see one.
  • Check conditions before island trips, especially July–October when monsoon swells can cancel or delay boats with little notice.
  • Signal is weak on outer islands like Ko Adang and parts of Tarutao — download offline maps and screenshot key numbers before you leave the mainland.
  • Tell someone your plan— which boat, which island, and roughly when you'll be back — especially for overnight camping trips in the national park.
  • Swim with caution— currents can be stronger than they look between islands; stick to sheltered beaches when swimming alone.

General travel safety

Heat & sun

Satun sits near the equator — hydrate often, use sunscreen, and avoid strenuous hikes (like Phu Pha Phet cave) during the hottest midday hours.

Save numbers offline

Add 1155 and 1669 to your phone contacts before you travel, and note this page's numbers somewhere that works without signal.

Travel insurance

Confirm your travel insurance covers boat transfers and remote-island evacuation before island-hopping trips.

Language help

If a Thai-language line (191, 199) doesn't understand you, hang up and call the Tourist Police (1155) instead — they can relay to local services in English.

This is a visitor guide, not an official emergency service. Numbers and addresses on this page were checked against official Thai government and embassy sources, but always verify locally if something looks out of date — services and phone systems do change.
